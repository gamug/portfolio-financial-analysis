# Skill: SEC Filing Free Cash Flow Yield (FCF Yield) Computation

This document outlines a specialized standard operating procedure (SOP) and prompt template designed for an AI assistant (like Claude or Gemini) to accurately extract financial data from SEC filings and compute **Free Cash Flow Yield (FCF Yield)**.

It integrates advanced corporate finance concepts, valuation theory, and forensic accounting principles to address stock-based compensation dilution, lease treatments, and capital intensity.

---

## 1. System Persona & Core Objectives
When activated, the AI should adopt the persona of a **Senior Forensic Financial Analyst**. The core objectives are:
1. **Precise Extraction**: Correctly locate and extract operating cash flow, capital expenditures, revenue, shares outstanding, and other necessary adjustment line items from SEC filings (typically Form 10-K).
2. **Methodological Rigor**: Differentiate clearly between **Equity FCF Yield** (levered FCF relative to market capitalization) and **Enterprise FCF Yield** (unlevered FCF relative to enterprise value), applying the correct formula based on the analytical scope.
3. **Forensic Valuation Gates**: Address valuation distortions, such as share dilution from stock-based compensation (SBC), lumpy capital expenditures, operating lease capitalization, and cash-to-accrual timing differences.
4. **Value-Trap Diagnostics**: Analyze whether a high FCF Yield indicates an undervalued gem or a "value trap" caused by temporary working capital manipulation, severe underinvestment, or a deteriorating business cycle.

---

## 2. Mathematical Foundations & Rationale

At its core, **Free Cash Flow Yield** is a valuation metric that measures the free cash flow generated by a company relative to its market value. It is the cash-flow-based equivalent of the Earnings Yield ($E/P$) and is widely considered a superior metric because cash flows are less susceptible to accounting manipulations than net income.

Depending on whether the analysis is conducted at the **Equity** or **Firm (Enterprise)** level, the AI must apply the appropriate formula:

### Path A: Equity Free Cash Flow Yield (Levered Yield)
Equity FCF Yield measures the cash flow available to common equity holders (after debt service) relative to the market value of the equity.

$$FCF\ Yield_{\text{Equity}} = \frac{\text{Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)}}{\text{Market Capitalization}}$$

Alternatively, on a per-share basis:

$$FCF\ Yield_{\text{Equity}} = \frac{\text{FCFE per Share}}{\text{Share Price}}$$

*Where:*
*   **FCFE** is standardly calculated as:
    $$\text{FCFE} = \text{Operating Cash Flow (OCF)} - \text{Capital Expenditures (CapEx)}$$
*   **Market Capitalization** is defined as:
    $$\text{Market Capitalization} = \text{Common Shares Outstanding} \times \text{Share Price}$$
*   **FCFE per Share** is defined as:
    $$\text{FCFE per Share} = \frac{\text{FCFE}}{\text{Weighted Average Diluted Shares Outstanding}}$$
*   **Share Price** is the closing stock price on or near the fiscal year-end date.

### Path B: Enterprise Free Cash Flow Yield (Unlevered / Firm Yield)
Enterprise FCF Yield measures the cash flow generated by the firm’s core operations (available to both debt and equity holders) relative to the total value of the operating enterprise (Enterprise Value).

$$FCF\ Yield_{\text{Enterprise}} = \frac{\text{Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF)}}{\text{Enterprise Value (EV)}}$$

*Where:*
*   **FCFF (Unlevered FCF)** is calculated from operating profit as:
    $$\text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{CapEx} - \Delta\text{Working Capital} - \text{Other changes in Net Operating Assets (NOA)}$$
*   **Enterprise Value (EV)** is defined as:
    $$\text{EV} = \text{Market Capitalization} + \text{Total Debt} - \text{Cash and Cash Equivalents}$$
    *   *Total Debt* includes short-term debt, current portion of long-term debt, and long-term debt.
    *   *Cash and Cash Equivalents* should include short-term marketable securities.

## 3. Data Extraction Protocol (SEC Filings)

Public companies list their financial statements in **Form 10-K (Annual Report)** under **Item 8: Financial Statements and Supplementary Data**. The AI must follow this extraction path:

1.  **Operating Cash Flow (OCF)**: Sourced from the **Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows**. Look for "Net cash provided by operating activities."
2.  **Capital Expenditures (CapEx)**: Sourced from the **Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows** under "Cash flows from investing activities." Typically listed as "Purchases of property, plant, and equipment" or "Additions to property, equipment, and software."
3.  **Common Shares Outstanding**: 
    *   *For Market Capitalization*: Sourced from the **Consolidated Balance Sheets** (shares outstanding at the exact fiscal year-end date) or the **Cover Page** of the Form 10-K (which lists shares outstanding as of the latest practicable date). The Balance Sheet year-end figure is preferred for consistency with the financial statements.
    *   *For Per-Share Metrics*: Sourced from the **Consolidated Statements of Operations** as "Weighted average shares outstanding - Diluted."
4.  **Cash, Debt, and Lease Liabilities (for EV)**: Sourced from the **Consolidated Balance Sheets**.
    *   *Cash and Cash Equivalents* and *Short-term Investments/Marketable Securities*.
    *   *Short-term Debt / Commercial Paper / Current Portion of Long-term Debt*.
    *   *Long-term Debt / Senior Notes*.
5.  **Share Price**: Sourced from external market data corresponding to the close of the fiscal year-end date (since SEC filings rarely provide the exact closing stock price for that specific day).
6.  **Addressing Restatements**: Always extract the financial figures for any historical year from the *most recent* filing available that covers that year to capture retrospective restatements, reclassifications, or discontinued operations.

## 4. Execution Workflow (Step-by-Step)

The AI must execute and document the calculation using the following seven steps:

```
[INPUT SEC Filings & Market Data] -> [STEP 1: Scope Audit] -> [STEP 2: Path Selection] -> [STEP 3: Sourced Extraction] -> [STEP 4: FCF Yield Calculation] -> [STEP 5: Forensic Valuation Gates] -> [STEP 6: Value-Trap Diagnostic] -> [STEP 7: Analytical Summary]
```

### Step 1: Target & Scope Audit
*   Identify the target company, the target fiscal years, the filing date, and the specific market data utilized (specifically the share price on the fiscal year-end date).

### Step 2: Path Selection
*   Declare whether **Path A (Equity FCF Yield)** or **Path B (Enterprise FCF Yield)** is being calculated, justifying why. (If the user does not specify, default to **Path A** but briefly outline the EV-level yield as an alternative).

### Step 3: Sourced Financial & Market Data Extraction
*   Locate and extract all required variables.
*   **Mandatory Sourcing Note**: Explicitly quote the exact table name, section of the filing, and line item name for every extracted financial variable. Sourced market prices must be dated.
*   **Pre-computation Gate**: Verify that **Market Capitalization > 0** (or **Enterprise Value > 0**). If market value is zero or negative, halt the process.

### Step 4: Step-by-Step FCF Yield Calculation
Perform and document the mathematics explicitly:
1.  **Calculate Free Cash Flow ($FCF$)**: Show the raw inputs and result (e.g., $FCFE = OCF - CapEx$).
2.  **Calculate Valuation Denominator**: Show the calculation for Market Capitalization or Enterprise Value.
3.  **Divide FCF by Value Denominator**: Show the raw fraction.
4.  **Output Final FCF Yield**: Convert to percentage and round to exactly two decimal places (e.g., $0.07845 \rightarrow 7.85\%$).

### Step 5: Forensic Valuation Gates (Addressing Distortions)
Identify and discuss potential distortions in the calculated yield:
*   **Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Dilution**: SBC is added back to OCF as a non-cash expense. However, it represents a real economic cost that dilutes shareholders. In forensic analysis, if a company has substantial SBC, the reported FCF is "inflated." The AI should calculate an **SBC-Adjusted FCF Yield**:
    $$\text{SBC-Adjusted FCF} = \text{FCF} - \text{Stock-Based Compensation}$$
    Compare this adjusted yield to the reported yield to show the impact of share dilution.
*   **Lumpy CapEx**: Capital expenditures can be highly lumpy. A year with low CapEx (underinvestment) will show an artificially high FCF Yield, whereas a heavy investment year will show a depressed yield. Examine the trend over 3 years to see if CapEx was abnormally low in the target year.
*   **Operating Leases (ASC 842 / IFRS 16)**: Under operating lease standards, lease liabilities are kept on the balance sheet but lease payments are fully inside OCF. If a competitor uses capital leases or debt-financed purchases, their investments are in CapEx, reducing their reported FCF. High operating lease intensity can artificially inflate FCF relative to capital-heavy peers.

### Step 6: Value-Trap Diagnostic
Analyze whether a high FCF Yield is sustainable:
*   **Cash Conversion Check**: Compute the Cash Conversion Ratio ($\text{FCF} / \text{Net Income}$). If Net Income is positive but FCF is negative, the yield is zero/negative, signaling poor earnings quality. Conversely, if FCF is massively higher than Net Income, check if the company is stretching accounts payable or aggressively collecting receivables, which are non-recurring working capital tailwinds.
*   **Underinvestment Warning**: Is the high yield driven by a severe cut in CapEx? Cutting CapEx boosts short-term FCF Yield but degrades long-term competitive advantage and earning power.

### Step 7: Forensic Analytical Summary
*   Provide a concise synthesis of the FCF Yield trend.
*   State whether the company is truly undervalued based on cash generation, or if the high yield masks structural headwinds, dilution, or unsustainable working capital maneuvers.

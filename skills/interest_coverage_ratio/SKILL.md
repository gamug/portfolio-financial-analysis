# Skill: SEC Filing Interest Coverage Ratio Computation

This document outlines a specialized standard operating procedure (SOP) and prompt template designed for an AI assistant (like Claude or Gemini) to accurately extract financial data from SEC filings and compute the **Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR)**.

It integrates advanced corporate finance and credit analysis principles to address GAAP distortions, interest-netting anomalies, lease capitalization differences (ASC 842 vs. IFRS 16), and capitalized interest treatments.

---

## 1. System Persona & Core Objectives
When activated, the AI should adopt the persona of a **Senior Credit Risk Analyst / Forensic Accountant**. The core objectives are:
1. **Precise Extraction**: Correctly locate and extract Operating Income (EBIT), Gross Interest Expense, Depreciation & Amortization (D&A), and relevant Note disclosures from SEC filings (typically Form 10-K/10-Q).
2. **Gross vs. Net Rigor**: Strictly identify and adjust for the "Interest-Netting Trap," where companies offset interest expense with interest income on the face of the income statement.
3. **Forensic Adjustments**: Correct for capitalized interest, operating lease discrepancies (US GAAP vs. IFRS), and non-cash expense adjustments (such as Stock-Based Compensation).
4. **Credit Risk Diagnosis**: Evaluate the company's financial distress and solvency risk using standard rating agency thresholds (e.g., S&P / Moody's interest coverage tiers), flagging potential "Zombie Company" status.

---

## 2. Mathematical Foundations & Corporate Finance Logic

The Interest Coverage Ratio measures a company’s ability to pay interest on its outstanding debt. A higher ratio indicates greater financial margin and lower default risk. Depending on the analytical lens (strict solvency vs. short-term liquidity), analysts use three distinct formulas:

### Path A: Operating Interest Coverage Ratio (EBIT / Gross Interest) - Standard Metric
This is the primary metric used for long-term solvency analysis. It compares the operating earnings generated by core business operations against contractual interest obligations.

$$\text{Interest Coverage Ratio (EBIT)} = \frac{\text{EBIT (Operating Income)}}{\text{Gross Interest Expense}}$$

*   **Financial Logic**: Using Operating Income (EBIT) ensures that interest payments are compared only against profits generated from ongoing operations, excluding non-operating windfalls or volatile investment income.
*   **The "Gross" Rule**: The denominator must represent **Gross Interest Expense** before any interest income offset. Contractual debt service does not decrease just because a company has short-term cash yielding interest.

### Path B: EBITDA Interest Coverage Ratio - Liquidity Metric
Often utilized by lenders and rating agencies for highly levered or capital-intensive firms, this ratio serves as a near-term cash-flow proxy.

$$\text{Interest Coverage Ratio (EBITDA)} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Gross Interest Expense}}$$

$$\text{Where: } \text{EBITDA} = \text{EBIT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Amortization}$$

*   **Financial Logic**: Since Depreciation and Amortization are non-cash expenses, this path measures cash-like operating margins available to cover interest.
*   **Limitation**: This overstates long-term coverage because capital expenditures (which D&A represents over time) are real cash requirements that cannot be ignored indefinitely.

### Path C: Cash Interest Coverage Ratio (FCF-Based)
The most conservative credit metric, showing the actual cash left over to meet interest payments after reinvesting in the business.

$$\text{Cash Interest Coverage Ratio} = \frac{\text{Operating Cash Flow (OCF)} + \text{Cash Interest Paid} + \text{Cash Taxes Paid}}{\text{Cash Interest Paid}}$$

## 3. Sourcing & Data Extraction Protocol (Form 10-K)

The AI must follow a structured extraction pathway under **Item 8: Financial Statements and Supplementary Data** to avoid common SEC filing extraction errors:

1.  **Operating Income (EBIT)**:
    *   *Source*: **Consolidated Statements of Operations** (or *Statements of Income*).
    *   *Line Item*: "Operating income", "Operating profit", or "Income from operations".
    *   *Trap*: Do not use "Net Income" or "Income before Income Taxes." These contain non-operating items, interest expense itself, and tax effects. EBIT must reflect earnings *before* finance costs and taxes.
2.  **Gross Interest Expense**:
    *   *Source*: Examine the **Consolidated Statements of Operations**. 
    *   *Trap (Netting)*: If the statement lists "Interest expense, net" or "Interest income (expense), net," the AI **must** navigate to the footnotes.
    *   *Footnote Search*: Search for **"Note - Debt"**, **"Note - Long-Term Debt"**, or **"Note - Supplementary Income Statement Information"** to extract **Gross Interest Expense** and **Gross Interest Income** separately.
    *   *Statement of Cash Flows Check*: As a triangulation step, check the bottom of the *Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows* under "Supplemental Disclosures of Cash Flow Information" for "Cash paid for interest" (this is the actual cash interest paid, which may differ from the accrual expense on the Income Statement).
3.  **Depreciation and Amortization (D&A)**:
    *   *Source*: Sourced from the **Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows** (under Cash flows from operating activities adjustments).
    *   *Trap*: Do not extract D&A from the Income Statement alone, as companies often embed depreciation inside "Cost of Goods Sold" or "SG&A" and do not display it as a standalone line item.
4.  **Addressing Restatements**:
    *   Always extract comparative historical data (e.g., FY2021 and FY2022 figures) from the *most recent* available filing (e.g., the FY2023 10-K). This ensures retrospectively restated, corrected, or discontinued operations adjustments are reflected.

## 4. Forensic Adjustments & GAAP/IFRS Distortions

A standard calculation can be highly misleading. The AI must execute and document these four forensic filters:

### 1. Capitalized Interest Adjustment (ASC 835-20)
*   **The Issue**: Under US GAAP, companies must capitalize interest on long-term construction projects (e.g., factories, real estate, software development). This capitalized interest bypasses the Income Statement (reducing reported Interest Expense and artificially boosting EBIT) and is added to PP&E on the Balance Sheet.
*   **The Correction**: Capitalized interest represents a real cash outflow to lenders. The AI must search the Debt or PP&E notes for "Capitalized Interest" and adjust the denominator:
    $$\text{Adjusted Interest Expense} = \text{Reported Interest Expense} + \text{Capitalized Interest}$$
    $$\text{Adjusted EBIT} = \text{Reported EBIT} - \text{Capitalized Interest} \text{ (if capitalized interest was not already expensed)}$$

### 2. Operating Lease Distortion (ASC 842 vs. IFRS 16)
*   **The Issue**: Under **US GAAP (ASC 842)**, operating leases are expensed as a single operating lease cost inside Operating Income (reducing EBIT), with no lease interest recognized in Interest Expense. Under **IFRS 16**, all leases are capitalized, generating lease depreciation (in operating costs) and lease interest (inside Interest Expense).
*   **The Distortion**: An IFRS company will appear to have a *higher EBIT* and a *higher Interest Expense* than an identical US GAAP peer, artificially compressing its standard Interest Coverage Ratio.
*   **The Correction**: For cross-border comparisons, check the Lease Note. If performing a US-GAAP to IFRS reconciliation, note how lease interest affects the comparability of interest coverage.

### 3. Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Addback
*   **The Issue**: SBC is a non-cash expense that reduces reported Operating Income (EBIT). High-growth technology firms often have massive SBC.
*   **The Correction**: For a cash-coverage view, analysts can calculate **SBC-Adjusted EBIT**:
    $$\text{SBC-Adjusted EBIT} = \text{EBIT} + \text{Stock-Based Compensation}$$
    *The AI must flag that while this improves the coverage ratio, it masks shareholder dilution costs.*

### 4. The "Zombie Company" Gate
*   **The Issue**: If a company’s EBIT is negative, the resulting Interest Coverage Ratio is negative. A negative ratio (e.g., -2.5x) is mathematically uninformative.
*   **The Protocol**: The AI must halt calculations if EBIT is negative, declaring the company has **failed to cover its interest obligations from operations**, indicating extreme solvency risk or a "Zombie Company" status (dependent on debt to keep operating).

## 5. Execution Workflow (Step-by-Step)

The AI must execute and document the calculation using the following seven steps:

```
[INPUT SEC Filings] -> [STEP 1: Identify Parameters] -> [STEP 2: Extract Data & Citations] -> [STEP 3: Address Netting & Restatements] -> [STEP 4: Apply Forensic Adjustments] -> [STEP 5: Calculate Ratios (EBIT & EBITDA)] -> [STEP 6: Solvency Risk Diagnosis] -> [STEP 7: Analytical Summary]
```

### Step 1: Target & Scope Identification
*   Specify the company name, target fiscal years, and document source.

### Step 2: Data Extraction with Source Citations
*   Locate EBIT, Gross Interest Expense, D&A, and Capitalized Interest. Quote the exact table names and sections of the 10-K/10-Q where each variable resides.

### Step 3: Interest Expense Reconciliation
*   Explicitly check whether the reported interest expense on the Statement of Operations is **Gross** or **Net**. Reconcile any discrepancies using the footnotes or Statement of Cash Flows.

### Step 4: Application of Forensic Filters
*   Check for Capitalized Interest and Stock-Based Compensation. Calculate the Adjusted EBIT and Adjusted Interest Expense if applicable.

### Step 5: Dual Path Calculation
*   Compute **Path A (EBIT-based Interest Coverage)** and **Path B (EBITDA-based Interest Coverage)** for each fiscal year.
*   Show all mathematical substitutions and round final ratios to **two decimal places** (e.g., `4.57x`).

### Step 6: Solvency Risk Tiers & Credit Diagnosis
Compare calculated EBIT-based ratios against credit risk categories:
*   **Ratio > 3.0x**: Healthy coverage; low short-to-medium term credit risk.
*   **Ratio 1.5x to 3.0x**: Acceptable, but highly vulnerable to macroeconomic downturns or rising interest rates.
*   **Ratio 1.0x to 1.5x**: Highly levered / high credit risk. Highly sensitive to operational disruptions.
*   **Ratio < 1.0x**: **Insolvent Operating Margin**. The firm is not generating enough cash from operations to service its debt (potential "Zombie Company").

### Step 7: Forensic Analytical Summary
*   Interpret trends (e.g., is coverage deteriorating due to rising debt balances, higher benchmark interest rates, or shrinking operating margins?).
*   Flag if EBITDA-based coverage is masking a dangerously low EBIT coverage due to extreme capital intensity (high depreciation).

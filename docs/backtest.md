# Backtest and GUARD

The daily backtest of `cycle` + `quant` on the final pilot database (`pilot_a3`), and GUARD, the
four-layer risk overlay that is measured on top of one of the arms. This file is the method and
the limitations; the results themselves are in
[`docs/backtest_results.md`](backtest_results.md) / [`docs/backtest_results.json`](backtest_results.json),
both generated by `python -m cycle.backtest export` from the run copies (never typed by hand).

The design is fixed by `BACKTEST_SPEC.md` (reviewer A, 2026-10-28) and `FINISH_REPO.md`; the
requirements are `FR-016` (daily backtest) and `FR-017` (GUARD) in `.specify/memory/SPEC.md`.
The pre-registered defensive configuration search, its selection and the H1–H3 holdout test are
in [`docs/champion_results.md`](champion_results.md); the pre-registration itself is
[`docs/champion_preregistration.md`](champion_preregistration.md) and the committed selection is
[`docs/champion_selection.json`](champion_selection.json).

### 1.1 The FUNDAMENTAL scorer and its cohort

The reported FUNDAMENTAL scores are the LLM run's **configuration A**: `LLM_THINKING=disabled`,
`temperature = 0`, the anonymized `anon-v1` prompts, and the model `deepseek-flash` resolved once
per run to **`DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash [thinking=disabled]`** (the resolved name, not the moving alias,
is stored in every run record and every row's `inputs_json`; one prompt hash, `c1de2b6e…`). The
two scoring stages made **no fallback** and no failed filing: **9,896** scored rows and **129**
`unscored-pillar-coverage-v1` rows (the D1 rule, no model call). The two stages cost **19.2651 USD**
(5.3488 + 13.9163) and made 107,745 calls; the pilot's A1-vs-A2 repeat had a mean absolute
difference of **2.0** points (max 5, r **0.965**). Reviewer A's independent check puts the scores
at Pearson **0.92** / Spearman **0.92** against the OpenCode simulation (n = 669 + 727) and at
Pearson **0.838–0.856** against the rule scorer in every window, H1–H3 included. The anonymized
prompts carry no ticker, company name, CIK, fiscal year or calendar date; absolute currency amounts
that are not pillar metrics are omitted (`docs/model_fixes.md` REPLAY-5).

**The cohort fix.** `_fundamental_normalization` now z-scores the FUNDAMENTAL totals over the
decision date's **universe members only**, the set `data.active_universe` resolves and the ranking
already uses; before the fix it used every asset with a latest row. At the P1/P2a dates those
extra rows were 58–66 (P1) and 75 (P2a) non-members — future members (lookahead) and former members
whose period end was up to 1,265 days old. The `normalize` writer and the `rank` reader still share
the one function, so a non-member row is neither normalized nor written; TECHNICAL and VALORIZATION
were already member-only. The fix is validated at every decision date of every reported run: the
normalization cohort has **0** non-members. GUARD's parameters were calibrated on 2019–2021 member
returns, not on scores, and are unchanged.

## 2. Calendar and windows

The calendar is D1: executions `E_k = D1 + 21k` NYSE sessions (`kg_schema.trading_calendar`), with
`E_k < D_end`; the decision is taken at the close of the session **before** `E_k`, the order
executes at the close of `E_k`, and the return of `E_k` is earned by the old weights.

## 3. Arms

One shared selection and one shared Markowitz target per decision date:

| arm | book |
|---|---|
| `MARKOWITZ_BASE` | min-variance over the whole eligible universe (`exclude_hard_vetoed = False`), no cycle selection |
| `TOPN_MV_NOGUARD` | cycle Top-30 (vetoes active), min-variance on the 30-name sub-covariance, GUARD off (alerts recorded as `ARM_DISABLED`) |
| `TOPN_MV_GUARD_NO_NLP` | same as `TOPN_MV_NOGUARD` plus GUARD layers 1, 3 and 4 |
| `TOPN_MV_GUARD` | same as `TOPN_MV_NOGUARD` plus all four layers — **not run**: layer 2 (news) has no admissible sentiment input (§9, F1) |

Benchmark (context only): `SP500_EW_INTERNAL`, the equal-weight total return of the members as of
the previous session that are priced on the session.

Core: min-variance, `cov_estimator = ledoit_wolf_cc`, `lookback 756`, floor 740 observations, top-30,
name cap `1.5/n_held`, sector cap 0.30, no turnover cap, fully invested at the decision (cash 0).

**What "30 names" means here (the fixed invariant).** At every decision `cycle` selects exactly 30
valid candidates (`cycle_ranking.selected = 1`, all in `ids(model_id)`; a shortfall raises
`TooFewCandidates` and aborts). `quant optimize --cycle-run-id` then solves the long-only
min-variance problem on that 30 × 30 sub-covariance with **upper** bounds only
(`0 ≤ w_i ≤ 0.05`, sector `≤ 0.30`, `min_name_weight = 0.0`, no floor). A corner solution assigns
exactly zero to some selected names, so the book holds the **positive** weights of the 30-name
sub-covariance — 20–26 names in the reported LLM runs (P1 20–24, P3 21–25, P2 21–26); the rule
repeat spans 20–24.
This is the approved reading of FINISH_REPO §8/§12.8 (decision 2026-10-31: keep quant's
formulation, no floor); it is checked as "30 selected candidates, `min_var_topn` optimized over
their sub-covariance" and not as "30 positive weights in `quant_position`".

### Formation rule

The book forms at the first scheduled decision date whose cycle selection succeeds with 30 valid
names. The schedule keeps its anchor: a refusal moves the formation, never the grid. All arms and
the benchmark are evaluated from the same formation date. A refusal **after** formation aborts the
run; it is never a silent skip.

## 4. Daily loop, costs, cash and exits

Per session `t` and arm: (1) mark to market with the stored total return
(`r = exp(tr_log_return) − 1` from `quant_return_daily`), cash growing by the French RF; (2) execute
the one order formed at the previous close at the close of `t`; (3) GUARD signals with data up to
`t`; (4) a decision date re-runs the pipeline; (5) one net target for `t+1`. An order formed at `t`
is never cancelled or recomputed.

- **Costs**: `10 bps × Σ|w_target − w_drifted|`, one cost per net execution, charged on the
  execution session's return. Cash never pays. A rebalance and a GUARD action on the same session
  produce one net target and one cost.
- **Cash** earns French's daily `RF` (`FF_RF_DAILY`, fraction of one session). The file ends
  2026-08-31; a later session carries the last value, and the number of carried sessions is
  recorded on each run (`rf_carried_sessions`, 0 in every window here).
- **Exits from the panel**: a held asset with no total return on `t` (delisted, halted, left the
  data) is valued at its last close and its weight moves to cash at that close. The return panel
  now contains every point-in-time member on any session of the window, leavers included, so a
  name that leaves inside a window has a return series up to its exit (before the fix it had
  none and could never be held). There is no Shumway delisting adjustment (limitation 3); the
  corrective batch is superseded and no sensitivity from it is used here.

## 5. GUARD

GUARD is an **overlay on the NOGUARD twin**: at the close of `t` the reference weights are the
NOGUARD arm's weights for `t+1` (its drifted weights on an ordinary session, the new Markowitz
target on a decision date). GUARD's target for `t+1` is the reference with the cut names at zero,
then scaled by `s`. An order is sent only when that target differs from the GUARD arm's own drifted
weights, so with no cut and `s = 1` the GUARD arm holds exactly what NOGUARD holds and never
rebalances on its own. A re-entering name returns to its reference (NOGUARD) weight. A rebalance
never clears an alert or a cooldown.

| layer | source (labelled "inspired by") | rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kaminski & Lo (2014), Def. 1 | for each asset: `S_i(J) = Σ` of its last `J` simple returns; IN → OUT when `S_i(J) < −γ` (weight 0, to cash); OUT → IN when the asset's own one-session return `≥ δ`. No cooldown. While OUT the asset's own return is tracked, not cash's. Fewer than `J` real returns: no signal. |
| 2 | Tetlock (2007); Loughran & McDonald (2011) | **off** — see limitation 4: `nlp.db`'s labels come from `gamug/FinBERT-financial-news`, whose training pool includes 2022–2026, so the model is not admissible for a 2022–2025 backtest. |
| 3 | Moreira & Muir (2017) | `s_σ = min(1, σ*/σ̂₂₁)`, `σ̂₂₁` the annualized std (ddof = 1) of the last 21 daily returns of the reference weights held fixed (ex-ante, no feedback). |
| 4 | Grossman & Zhou (1993) via Yang & Zhong (2012) | `s_DD = clip(m (W_t − α M_t) / W_t, 0, 1)`, `W_t` the arm's NAV, `M_t` its running maximum since formation (which starts at the initial capital, 1.0). |

Combination: (1) layers 1 and 2 cut names to 0; (2) the cut weight goes to cash; (3) the remaining
equity weights are scaled by `s = min(1, s_σ, s_DD)` — the **minimum, never the product** — and the
rest goes to cash; (4) caps are never relaxed. AVANCE E1's adaptation (`m(1 − α) ≥ 1`) is what
makes the exposure exactly 1 at a new maximum.

Layer 3's `σ̂₂₁` is measured on the **reference weights** — the NOGUARD twin's weights for `t+1`
(BACKTEST_SPEC §8.1): its drifted weights on an ordinary session, the new Markowitz target on a
decision date (when the twin's own final target for `t+1` is the target it just formed). It is
therefore *not* the arm's own realized post-GUARD book: on a rebalance date the reference is the
target while the arm still holds the pre-trade book until the close, and a name cut by layer 1
leaves the reference too. This is the declared, spec-fixed definition of the denominator; it is
stated here so a reader of the series knows which weights it used.

Output per session: the alerts (layer, asset or portfolio, value, threshold, state change) and the
action per arm (`APPLIED` | `ARM_DISABLED` | `NOT_HELD` | `SUPERSEDED`), persisted in
`guard_alert` / `guard_action`. When layers 3 and 4 both emit a `SCALE` alert on the same session,
**both** rows are recorded `APPLIED` even though only the smaller of the two is the binding term of
`s = min(s_σ, s_DD)`; `n_guard_actions` therefore counts alerts applied, not binding constraints.

### What GUARD did in these runs (measured)

The numbers below are recomputed from the run copies by `scripts/backtest_guard_behaviour.py`
(aggregates only; evidence `out/a2/guard_behaviour_llm.log`).

**Layer 4 is active on almost every session.** With `m = 1/(1 − α)` the exposure is exactly 1 only
at a new NAV maximum, and the formation cost alone puts the arm below its maximum at the first
close. Sessions with `s_DD < 1`: **P1 195/196, P3 151/192, P2 145/150**. The arm is below its
initial capital, before the first close above it, for **1, 1 and 126 sessions** of P1, P3 and P2
respectively; every one of those has `s_DD < 1`. The first new high is 2022-01-04, 2023-10-30 and
2025-08-20; the stored value matches `m(W − α M)/W` with a maximum absolute error of **0.0** in
every run.

**Layer 1 whipsaws by construction.** A re-entry needs one session's return `≥ δ` while the same
name's `S(21)` can still be `< −γ`, so the next session can stop it again. APPLIED re-entries /
followed by another stop at any later session / followed by another stop within two sessions:
**P1 142/113/73 · P3 30/20/16 · P2 61/45/30**. This is Kaminski & Lo's Def. 1 (a one-period
re-entry condition) as specified, applied every session; it is a property of the daily overlay,
not a coding defect. The state outlives the reference: a name stopped before a rebalance drops it
keeps its return history, its re-entry is recorded `NOT_HELD`, and if it returns it is held at its
reference weight (`docs/model_fixes.md` REPLAY-6).

**GUARD's own trades.** Sessions on which NOGUARD has no turnover but GUARD trades: **P1 186,
P3 181, P2 142**; the extra turnover is **11.69 / 4.52 / 5.68** and the cost **116.9 / 45.2 /
56.8 bps** over the window. There is no no-trade band: an order is sent whenever a target weight
moves by more than `1e-12` (BACKTEST_SPEC §4), so a re-entry or a scaler change on an otherwise
quiet session is traded.

**A negative mean makes a low-volatility arm's Sharpe worse.** In P1 every arm's mean daily excess
return is negative (BASE −6.04e−4, NOGUARD −1.30e−3, GUARD −1.32e−3); with a negative numerator a
lower volatility gives a more negative Sharpe, not a better one. GUARD's annualized volatility is
**13.73 %** against NOGUARD's **20.78 %**, and its Sharpe **−2.42** against **−1.57**; the P1 paired
Sharpe tests (Ledoit–Wolf p = **0.0024**, Jobson–Korkie/Memmel p = **0.0050**) therefore rank the
lower-volatility GUARD arm as worse. The protection measures are the volatility and drawdown
columns (P1 MDD 24.25 % against NOGUARD's 27.19 %), not the Sharpe comparison.

## 6. Statistics

Per arm and segment (P1, P3, P2, P2a, P2b, and the holdout H1/H2/H3): cumulative return, annualized return (geometric),
annualized volatility (252, ddof = 1), ex-post Sharpe on excess returns over the daily French RF,
max drawdown, turnover and cost. Paired tests on the same dates, for NOGUARD vs BASE and
GUARD_NO_NLP vs NOGUARD:

- **Jobson–Korkie** with Memmel's (2003) correction (`memmel_jk`);
- **Ledoit–Wolf (2008) studentized circular block bootstrap** of the Sharpe difference
  (`ledoit_wolf_sharpe_diff`, B = 4999, block length 5, fixed seed `20261028`) — in LW's structure,
  with plain Newey–West HAC studentization, not LW's own prewhitened version (limitation 2);
- **Newey–West HAC** t-test on the daily return difference (`hac_mean_diff`).

### Low power, stated in advance

Every Sharpe in the results file, the `SP500_EW_INTERNAL` benchmark included, subtracts the run's
daily `FF_RF_DAILY` rate: the arms from `backtest_summary`, the benchmark from the run copy's own
`risk_free_rate` rows (same curve, annualized rate / 252, carried forward — the rule the driver
used for the arms). The benchmark and the arms are therefore on the same excess-return basis.

Each design window holds **150–196 daily returns** per arm (P2 150, P3 192, P1 196); the holdout
windows hold **135–267** (H2 135, H1 261, H3 267). A Sharpe difference of 0.3 on a 150-session
sample is not distinguishable from zero at conventional levels; the paired tests are reported for
completeness, not as a decision rule. The P2a/P2b cuts hold ~50 and ~100 sessions and are even
weaker.

## 10. Limitations, each with its measured effect

1. **The formation day is included in the statistics.** Its gross return is 0 and it pays the
   formation cost, identically in every arm, so it lowers every arm's cumulative return by the same
   ~10 bps and adds one zero-return observation to each series.
2. **The Ledoit–Wolf test is a studentized circular block bootstrap with plain Newey–West HAC**, not
   LW's prewhitened version. The p-value is therefore that of a slightly different (more
   conservative in long-memory cases) studentization; the sign and size of the statistic are
   unaffected.
3. **No Shumway delisting adjustment.** The return panel now contains every point-in-time member
   on any session of a window, leavers included, so a held asset that leaves is valued at its
   last stored close and its weight moves to cash; before this fix a leaver had no return series
   in the window and could never be held (survivorship / look-ahead bias; the correction is
   documented in `docs/model_fixes.md`). A delisting return (often very negative) is still not
   added, which biases every arm upward by the missing delisting return on the names that leave
   during a window. A corrective 18-run batch was kept under
   `/workspaces/thesis/scratch/final/out/pitfix/runs/`, but reviewer A's 2026-11-06 scope change
   supersedes it before any interpretation; no Shumway sensitivity is applied here.
4. **Layer 2 (news) is off.** `nlp.db`'s labels come from `gamug/FinBERT-financial-news`, whose
   training pool includes 2022–2026 (F1), so the model cannot be used for a 2022–2025 backtest; arm
   3 is not run and `TOPN_MV_GUARD_NO_NLP` is the GUARD result. Leavers have no news at all (F2).
5. **Deferred, declared**: the `SAFE_HAVEN` destination, the tangency portfolio, the monthly
   cadence and the cap-weighted benchmark. The reported arms are the ones above; nothing else is
   claimed.
6. **The 10 bps cost is the project's "by definition" value** (G2, no source found). It is a
   one-way cost on the traded fraction; at the observed turnover it costs each arm the amount in
   the `Cost bps` column of the results file.
7. **The calibration sample has little stress outside 2020** and still loses PIT member-sessions
   to missing returns. After the 26 L-42 leaver series are applied, the panel has no total return
   for **20,125 / 16,612 / 15,732** PIT member-sessions in 2019 / 2020 / 2021 (15.8 %, 13.0 % and
   12.4 % of the PIT membership); **11,820 / 6,670 / 2,331** of those have no `assets` row at all,
   the rest have a row but no return on the session (a halt or a missing bar). `gamma` is therefore
   measured on a panel that loses about a sixth of 2019. The leaver files restore 9,167 of the
   leaver returns; companies the gateway no longer serves and that are not in those files stay out.
8. **σ\* uses the equal-weight member portfolio as its proxy** for the Top-30 min-variance book,
   which cannot be formed before late 2021 (no risk model) or 2020 (no FUNDAMENTAL scores). If the
   Top-30 book is less volatile than the EW panel, layer 3 scales less often than it would with the
   book's own volatility.
9. **LLM lookahead (I1)**: the reported run is the LLM scorer (configuration A: resolved
   `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`, thinking disabled, temperature 0, `anon-v1` prompts); the rule-based run
   is the deterministic robustness repeat. The LLM sees only the anonymized metrics of the filing
   it scores, but its training data is not auditable — that is the lookahead this limitation
   declares. The reported scores have no fallback (9,896 scored, 129 D1-unscored) and cost 19.2651
   USD; the pilot's repeat (A1 vs A2) differed by 2.0 points on average (max 5).
10. **`unscored_max_share = 0.060`** instead of the 0.05 default, because P1's first two decisions
    carry 0.0574 (the table in §8). Effect: P1 forms at k = 0 instead of being refused; every other
    decision date is below 0.05 anyway.
11. **L-43 (multi-class market caps)**: the market-cap reader honours `cover_class_conversion`
    (reviewer B's `r11-d17`). Measured on the final database: Berkshire Hathaway's stored cap is
    1.5318–1.7546 × (class B count × class B close), i.e. class A is converted at 1500 (correct per
    MKT-03), not counted 1:1; Visa is **not** flagged `market_cap_incomplete_classes` in any of its
    27 stored values, and its own rates (class B 1.6181, class C 4.0) raise its share count 9.6 %
    above the raw sum of classes.

    The `excl_*` trigger of FINISH_REPO §8 is a conjunction: `(BRK understated OR Visa flagged)
    AND (BRK or V selected in a Top-30 book)`. The **first** clause is false — neither name is
    understated — so `excl_*` does not run whatever the selection does. For the record, `BRK.B` is
    selected in **5** of P1's 10 decisions (2022-03-03, 2022-04-01, 2022-05-03, 2022-06-02,

## 11. System v2 -- developed after v1

`docs/backtest_v2_plan.md` (reviewer A, 2026-11-04) fixes a second system, designed **after**
v1's results on all six windows: a SPY core-satellite blend (`core_weight`), Faber's 210-session
trend filter (`trend_filter`), GUARD applied to the whole book, a `MARKOWITZ_BASE_GUARD` arm and
a paired circular block bootstrap of the maximum-drawdown difference. The plan's §7 records the
implementation readings that were fixed before any v2 run.

The grid is 3 x 2 = 6 configurations (`c` in {0, 0.5, 0.7} x filter off/on) on the six windows,
36 runs, LLM scores, blend B0·T0 and the frozen GUARD parameters. V0 (`c = 0`, filter off)
**reproduces v1 exactly** on every window: summaries, tests, weights and GUARD actions, maximum
absolute difference 0.0. The choice is made by mean Top-N GUARD MDD over the six windows (plan
§4): **V3** (core 0.5, filter on), mean MDD 0.0781, lower than v1's in 5 of the six windows (all
but H1). Every v2 result is **in-sample** -- no unseen data remains -- and no significance is
claimed beyond the reported tests. The full grid, the tests, the filter dates and the V0 identity
check are in [`docs/backtest_v2_results.md`](backtest_v2_results.md), generated by
`scripts/backtest_v2_export.py` from the run copies.

The SPY total-return file is a scratch artifact (sha256 `58b91870...`), refused unless its sha256
matches `manifest.json` beside it; only window aggregates are committed. v1's results and docs are
unchanged, and no model call was made in this round.
